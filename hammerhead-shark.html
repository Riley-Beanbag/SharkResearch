HAMMERHEAD SHARKS

Shark Species Profile

Scientific Classification

Family: Sphyrnidae

Order: Carcharhiniformes

Number of living species: 9 recognised species

Conservation status: Varies between species

Physical Characteristics

Hammerhead sharks are recognised by their unusual hammer-shaped heads, called cephalofoils. Their eyes and nostrils are positioned at the ends of their wide heads.

Habitat and Distribution

Hammerhead sharks live in tropical and warm temperate oceans around the world, different species inhabit coastal waters, continental shelves and open oceans. But they can also be found in some countries such as: the Bahamas, Australia, the United States, and Belize.

Diet

Hammerhead sharks eat fish, squid, octopuses, crustaceans and other marine animals. Some species specialise in eating stingrays too like: The Great Hammerhead by utilize their unique cephalofoil to detect the faint electrical signals emitted by stingrays buried in the sand, allowing them to locate and pin the prey.

Behaviour

Some hammerhead species form large schools during the day. Others are more solitary. They use their wide heads to help detect prey using their wide field of vision allowing them to nearly see 360 degrees basically meaninig they can see all around them and almost behind them.

Interesting Fact

The great hammerhead can grow to around 6 metres long! Since its head is wide they use it to their advantage by pinning stingrays against the seabed, allowing them to get to their prey. Literally a hammer to their prey treating them as a nail.

Conservation

Several hammerhead species are threatened by fishing, particularly because of demand for their fins. Conservation efforts and fishing regulations help protect their populations.
