Understanding Shark Habitats

Sharks inhabit oceans all around the world. Different species have adapted to different environments, from warm tropical reefs to the freezing waters of the Arctic.

Factors such as water temperature, depth, food availability and ocean conditions influence where sharks live.

1. Coral Reefs

Coral reefs are warm, shallow marine environments filled with a huge variety of marine life. They provide food and shelter for many shark species.

Example species: Blacktip Reef Shark, Grey Reef Shark.

2. Kelp Forests

Kelp forests are underwater ecosystems made up of large seaweeds called kelp. They provide shelter and feeding grounds for many marine animals.

Example species: Leopard Shark, Sevengill Shark.

3. Open Ocean

The open ocean is the vast area of seawater away from coastlines and the seabed. Some sharks travel enormous distances through these waters.

Example species: Whale Shark, Blue Shark, Shortfin Mako.

4. Coastal Waters

Coastal waters are areas of ocean close to land. They include bays, estuaries and shallow continental shelves. Many sharks use these areas as feeding and nursery habitats.

Example species: Lemon Shark, Great White Shark.

5. Deep Ocean

The deep ocean receives very little sunlight and has extremely high water pressure. Some shark species are specially adapted to survive in these conditions.

Example species: Goblin Shark, Frilled Shark.

6. Polar Waters

Polar waters are extremely cold environments found around the Arctic and Antarctic regions. Only specially adapted marine animals can survive in these conditions.

Example species: Greenland Shark.

What Affects Where Sharks Live?

  • Water temperature
  • Ocean depth
  • Food availability
  • Oxygen levels
  • Ocean currents
  • Breeding and nursery areas
