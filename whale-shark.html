Scientific Classification

Scientific name: Rhincodon typus

Family: Rhincodontidae

Order: Orectolobiformes

Average length: Around 4–12 metres

Conservation status: Endangered

Physical Characteristics

Whale sharks are the largest living fish in the world. They have broad, flattened heads, enormous mouths and grey-blue bodies covered in distinctive white spots and pale stripes.

Every whale shark has a unique pattern of spots, which researchers can use to identify individuals. Exactly how Zebras have their own unqiue patterns making it easier to idenify them

Habitat and Distribution

Whale sharks live in tropical and warm temperate oceans around the world. They are highly migratory and travel long distances between feeding areas.

In New Zealand, they are occasionally spotted in northern waters, particularly during warmer months.

Diet

Whale sharks are filter feeders. They eat plankton, tiny crustaceans, fish eggs and small fish. They filter food from large amounts of seawater using specialised structures in their gills.

Behaviour

Whale sharks are generally solitary animals, although groups can gather where food is plentiful. They can dive to great depths and travel thousands of kilometres during migrations.

Interesting Facts

Despite their enormous size, whale sharks are gentle filter feeders and are not considered a threat to people, allowing divers to go near them for a beautoful view of them as they swim past and sometimes feed.

Their unique spot patterns work a little like fingerprints, allowing scientists to recognise individual whale sharks.

Conservation

Whale sharks are classified as Endangered. They face threats from fishing, accidental capture and collisions with boats.

Research, marine protection areas and responsible wildlife tourism help support their conservation.
