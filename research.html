Shark Behaviour

Discover how sharks interact with their environment.

Explore Topic

Sharks use different behaviours to hunt, communicate, migrate and find food.

  • Sharks hunt using their strong senses to locate prey. They detect vibrations through their lateral line, electrical signals through their ampullae of Lorenzini, and use their excellent smell and eyesight to find food. They then use their speed, powerful tails and sharp teeth to catch their prey. 🦈
  • How do sharks migrate? Sharks migrate long distances to find food, warmer water and breeding areas. They use ocean currents, temperature and Earth's magnetic field to help navigate. 🦈
  • Do sharks interact with each other? Yes! Sharks interact through social behaviours such as swimming together, communicating through body movements and competing for food. Some species form groups, while others prefer living and hunting alone.

Shark Biology

Explore the science behind shark bodies.

Explore Topic

Sharks have special adaptations that help them survive.

  • Sharks breathe using gills that extract dissolved oxygen from water. Water enters through their mouths, passes over tiny blood-filled structures called gill filaments, and exits through their gill slits. Oxygen enters the bloodstream while carbon dioxide leaves it. This process allows sharks to breathe underwater without lungs.
  • Sharks detect movement using a sensory system called the lateral line. It runs along both sides of their bodies and contains specialised cells that detect vibrations, water pressure changes and nearby movement. Sharks also have ampullae of Lorenzini, which detect weak electrical fields produced by other living animals. These sensory systems help sharks navigate their surroundings and locate prey, even when they cannot see it.
